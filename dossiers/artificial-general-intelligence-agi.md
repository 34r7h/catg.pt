## Section 1. Orientation: what AI is, the role, the pipeline (lessons 1–4)

- **Definitions (lesson 1):** AI = recognizing recurring patterns, learning and making predictions/decisions from data; subfields named — machine learning, deep learning ("multiple layers of non-linear transformation"), NLP (linguistics + AI), computer vision, robotics.
- **Career map (lessons 2–3):** R&D, ML engineering, NLP, computer vision, robotics, ethics/public policy, AI consulting/advisory, education/training. Role sketches given for each: ML engineers build and deploy models; NLP specialists build language systems; ethics professionals ensure "fairness, transparency and accountability"; consultants identify opportunities and navigate ethical/regulatory considerations.
- **The AI-engineer pipeline (lesson 4) — the course syllabus in one lecture:** (1) define the stakeholder problem, (2) collect/preprocess data, (3) research, select and develop the right algorithms, (4) extract/engineer meaningful features, (5) deploy trained models to production against performance requirements, (6) monitor and retrain continuously (data and model drift), (7) collaborate with data scientists, software engineers and domain experts, (8) conduct research to stay current, (9) mind ethics — bias in data/models, privacy, societal impact, (10) follow a career roadmap. PDFs accompany every lecture.

## Section 2. Problem definition: stakeholders first (lessons 5–8)

- **Identify stakeholders (lesson 5):** everyone impacted by or with a vested interest — cast the net wide; **encourage open communication and active participation**: "one stakeholder who is silent… could become a bottleneck later on."
- **Understand needs (lesson 6):** open-ended questions ("what are your key requirements? main challenges? current pain points?"), ask for specific examples, run one-on-one interviews; gather process documents, datasets, reports.
- **Find the AI opportunity (lesson 7):** the overlap of *stakeholder need* × *what ML can solve* × *business problem* — automation, better decisions/user experience, or unlocking insights; prioritize issues "that offer the greatest potential for value creation"; **define measurable success metrics with stakeholders** up front; build prototypes/PoCs to demonstrate the technique, then get feedback on how close they are to needs.
- **Documentation & communication (lesson 8):** requirements doc on three parameters — **stakeholder requirements, business objectives, technical specifications**; keep stakeholders informed on progress, challenges and decision points, soliciting input at key milestones.
- **Worked example (lesson 8): radiology diagnostics.** Stakeholders = radiologists, IT, administrators, patients. Paint points: subtle abnormalities, time pressure, waiting times, access to specialized care. Three opportunities identified — automated image analysis, anomaly detection, decision support — addressed with CNNs for image analysis, deep learning for feature extraction, NLP; success measured as AI-assisted vs. human-radiologist diagnosis accuracy; prototype → pilot validation with radiologists on real imaging data → documented requirements → continuous communication.

## Section 3. Data collection & preprocessing (lessons 9–14)

- **Define data requirements (lesson 9)** from analysis objectives — identify variables, criteria and constraints; quality pass for missing values, duplicate entries, outliers, format inconsistencies.
- **Missing values (lesson 9):** mean/mode/median imputation; **k-nearest-neighbor imputation** (label/value from k similar training points); **regression imputation** (predict from complete variables — caveat: can overestimate correlation). **Duplicates:** remove whole-row or partial duplicates (Excel Remove Duplicates walkthrough).
- **Standardization & outliers (lesson 10):** date formats normalized to **ISO 8601** (31/12/2023 vs 2023-12-31); outliers = points beyond z ≈ −3/−4; **winsorization** reduces outlier effects without removing data.
- **Encoding, scaling, normalization (lesson 10):** one-hot / label / target encoding for categories; **simple feature scaling** (x/max — range changes, shape doesn't); **normalization** z = (x − μ)/σ changes the distribution shape to standard; (lesson 11 also cites the 1964 Cox technique as taught).
- **Text feature extraction (lesson 11):** **TF-IDF** — term frequency × inverse document frequency (log of ratio of total docs to docs containing the term), normalized to avoid bias toward long documents; output is a **sparse document-term matrix** feeding classification/clustering/retrieval (demo: "information technology" scores 0.77 in doc 1, 0 in doc 2). **Word embeddings:** dense low-dimensional vectors via Word2Vec, GloVe, FastText; sentence vectors become features feeding logistic regression/SVM/neural nets — embeddings capture semantic *and* syntactic similarity ("the boy played in the park" / "the bird sat in the tree").
- **Image feature extraction (lesson 12):** HOG (gradient-orientation histograms — pedestrian/face detection), SIFT (scale/rotation-invariant key points — matching, panorama stitching), SURF (fast box-filter variant), CNNs (edges → textures → object parts; pretrained **VGG, ResNet, MobileNet** as feature extractors), LBP (local texture), shape/texture histograms, edge features (segmentation/shape analysis), corner features (stitching, calibration). Choice depends on task requirements.
- **EDA + split (lesson 12):** visualize with histograms and scatterplots; split into train/validation/test — typical **60–80% / 10–20% / 10–20%**.
- **Worked examples (lessons 13–14): customer-review sentiment.** Validate against ground truth; bag-of-words matrix (rows = reviews, columns = vocabulary words) then TF-IDF weighting (satisfied/great reviews score high); pre-trained Word2Vec/GloVe embeddings map review words to dense vectors (unseen words → zero vector), sentence vectors feed the classifier; final EDA of label distributions before training.

## Section 4. Research, select and develop algorithms (lessons 15–37)

### Problem type → evaluation metrics (lessons 15–20)

- **Three output types** determine everything downstream: **classification** (discrete classes — sentiment positive/negative/neutral, cat/dog/bird), **regression** (continuous values), **clustering** (group similar points — customer segmentation, document clustering); plus anomaly detection ("patterns that do not conform") and other domain-specific outputs, each with tailored metrics — always weigh domain specifics and stakeholder requirements.
- **Classification metrics:** accuracy (% correctly classified), precision (TP / TP+FP), recall (TP / TP+FN), **F1 = harmonic mean of precision and recall**, FPR = FP/(FP+TN), all read together from the **confusion matrix**.
- **Regression metrics:** MSE, RMSE, and **R²** — "variance of the target variable explained by the model"; high R² = good model.
- **Clustering metrics (lessons 17–20, taught formula-by-formula):**
  - **Silhouette score** — similarity of an object to its own cluster vs. nearest other cluster; **≈1 = well matched to own cluster, ≈0 = on the decision boundary, high average = dense, well-separated clusters**; used to compare algorithms/parameter settings/k.
  - **Davies-Bouldin index** — average similarity between each cluster and its most similar cluster (centroid-based); **lower is better**; low = well-separated distinct clusters, high = overlap/unclear boundaries; complements silhouette.
  - **Adjusted Rand Index / Adjusted Mutual Information** — agreement between true labels and cluster assignments, adjusted for chance: **1 = perfect, 0 = random-like, <0 = worse than chance**; worked demo comparing k-means vs mean-shift (ARI 0.94 ≈ very similar clustering).

### Hyperparameter tuning (lessons 21–24)

- **Gradient descent & learning rate (lesson 21):** gradient = direction of steepest loss increase; move opposite it; **step size = learning-rate hyperparameter**; low rate = slower but more stable convergence; basis of SGD, mini-batch GD, Adam.
- **Hidden layers (lesson 22):** layers and neurons-per-layer are hyperparameters; deeper architectures capture more complex representations but cost more compute and raise overfitting risk (especially small data) and training time.
- **Regularization strength (lesson 23)** is the third headline hyperparameter; tuning = systematic search over a predefined space evaluated by cross-validation.
- **Search strategies (lessons 23–24):** **Grid search** — exhaustive over defined values, K-fold CV per combo, pick best, optional held-out test check; explodes with many/wide hyperparameters. **Random search** — samples from distributions (uniform for continuous, discrete for categorical) per iteration; explores high-dimensional spaces faster, standard baseline before Bayesian methods. **Bayesian optimization** — probabilistic model (typically **Gaussian processes**) + acquisition function (**expected improvement**) iteratively balances exploration vs exploitation; for expensive objectives (deep nets, large data). **Genetic algorithms** — population of parameter vectors, fitness evaluation, selection/crossover/mutation; for large, non-linear search spaces where classical methods fail.

### Model comparison, iteration, generalization (lessons 25–29)

- **Comparison discipline (lesson 25):** pick metrics matching the goal (accuracy/precision/recall/F1 for classification, MSE for regression); **validation set tunes and selects, test set only estimates final generalization — never use the test set for selection or tuning**; keep splits representative; sweep algorithms, architectures, preprocessing; weigh computational efficiency, interpretability, scalability alongside validation performance.
- **Iteration loop (lessons 26–27):** fine-tune hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, regularization, layers, units, dropout) → experiment with architectures → inject domain knowledge → monitor train/validation metrics and adjust; define stopping criteria; keep a record of every hyperparameter/metric/adjustment.
- **Generalization toolkit (lesson 28):** **dropout** — zero a fraction of neurons each iteration (common rate **0.2–0.5**), disabled (=0) at inference so all neurons fire; prevents co-adaptation, adds noise, often combined with L1/L2; **batch normalization** — normalize each layer's activations per batch (subtract batch mean ÷ std), use **running population statistics** at inference (+ epsilon against numerical instability); reduces internal covariate shift, stabilizes and accelerates training; **early stopping** to halt overfitting.
- **Learning-rate schedules (lesson 29):** fixed, step decay, exponential decay, adaptive (cyclical LR, warm-up); adjust initial rate/decay/step size; watch validation curves for over/underfitting and adapt.
- **Training/validation monitoring (lesson 29–30):** plot metrics vs. epochs (line/bar/scatter); **training error ≪ validation error → overfitting; both high → underfitting**; spot slow convergence/oscillation/plateauing → adjust LR, batch size; error analysis on high-loss samples, outliers via confusion matrix, precision-recall curves, residual plots; iterate incrementally and log every experiment.

### Interpretability & explainability (lessons 30–37)

- **Feature importance analysis (lessons 30–33):** train a model that exposes importance → compute scores (**decision tree:** impurity reduction at splits, Gini; **random forest:** average impurity/MSE decrease across trees; **GBM:** XGBoost, LightGBM, scikit-learn) → visualize (bar charts, heat maps, scatter vs. feature/target values) → interpret ("which features score highest? any *expected-important* feature scoring low?") → feed back into feature selection and engineering. Code walkthroughs given for `DecisionTreeClassifier`, random forest, and XGBoost (`feature_importances_` + bar chart).
- **Interpretability methods (lessons 34–35):** **PDP** (partial dependence — how one feature moves the prediction, marginalizing others), **LIME** (train a locally interpretable **surrogate** model on one prediction region — can be an SVM, decision list, etc.), inspecting **SVM support vectors** nearest the decision boundary, interactive visualization interfaces, and clear documentation of assumptions.
- **Attention-based explainability (lesson 36–37):** **text attention** — visualize attention weights to see which input tokens are attended to / most contribute to the output; **visual attention** — CNN attention maps highlight salient image regions; attention-matrix visualizations make deep-model decisions inspectable.
- **Worked example (lesson 37): sentiment analysis as text classification.** Literature review on papers → candidate algorithms (SVM, Naive Bayes, logistic regression, RNN, CNN, **BERT**) → tokenize/normalize/vectorize → split train/val/test → train and compare with accuracy, precision, recall, F1, **AUC-ROC** → fine-tune hyperparameters → final validation on val, test for the winner → deploy REST API serving predictions and stakeholder insights. The running thread: "your model can be only as good as the features that you selected."

## Section 5. Feature engineering (lessons 38–43)

- **Thesis (lesson 38):** "how good is your model depends on how good are your features" — understand the problem area and data peculiarities → explore/EDA → brainstorm as many features as possible → select the important ones → evaluate feature importance of the remainder → iterate with domain knowledge → validate.
- **Creation techniques (lesson 39):** feature scaling to zero mean / unit variance; aggregating related features; **polynomial features** (generate from combinations of originals to capture non-linearity); **feature interaction** (cross products, transformations that amplify/diminish effects); log transformations; **domain-engineered features** from intuition/expertise; **temporal features** (timestamp, moving average, seasonality/periodic patterns); **spatial features** (distance to key locations, geo-proximity clustering, area statistics); domain transformations to highlight signal and suppress noise; validate via cross-validation / hold-out.
- **Selection (lesson 40):** **univariate statistical tests** — chi-square (categorical feature vs categorical target), ANOVA F (numerical features across categories), mutual information (general association), scikit-learn `SelectKBest` demos (e.g., top 5); **RFE** (recursive feature elimination — iteratively drop the least important); **embedded importance** from random forest / gradient boosting; then cross-validate, vary the number of selected features, tune the model.
- **Dimensionality reduction (lesson 41):** **PCA** — components capturing maximum variance, chosen via explained-variance-ratio / cumulative explained variance plots; **Truncated SVD** (linear, sparse-safe); **t-SNE** for visualizing the reduced space and assessing cluster separation; then train/cluster on the reduced set — preserves information, aids visualization, improves efficiency.
- **Close the loop (lesson 42):** re-evaluate model-specific feature importance after reduction, feed business insights back into engineering, validate the final feature set on a separate test set for real-world performance.
- **Worked example (lesson 43): house-price prediction.** Create **price per square foot**; encode neighborhood (one-hot); polynomial features for size/age (non-linear relationships); find-and-impute missing values per feature; **scale square-feet vs bedrooms** so big-range features don't dominate; chi-square/ANOVA/mutual-information selection; RFE to rank and drop; PCA/t-SNE to compress dimensions and visualize separation; train linear regression, decision tree, ensembles on the reduced set with cross-validation → better accuracy and robustness.

## Section 6. Deployment (lessons 44–46)

- **Serialization (lesson 44):** convert the trained model's parameters/state into a loadable format. Formats taught: **pickle** (standard Python, handles most objects), **joblib** (efficient for large NumPy arrays, scikit-learn ecosystem), **ONNX** (open, cross-framework interoperability). Choice depends on environment, application requirements, scalability, infrastructure.
- **Serving infrastructure (lesson 45):** choose a deployment model (cloud — AWS etc., servers, edge, **Docker/Kubernetes** containers); provision compute for inference load; expose a **REST API endpoint**; **put pre-processing inside the endpoint** (validation, normalization, scaling, encoding exactly as in training) or the model won't work; engineer for latency and throughput via **batch processing, caching, async processing**; monitor latency/throughput/error rates/resource use in real time; **secure** with authentication and privacy compliance (**GDPR**); integrate into application workflow (update app code to call the endpoint and process returned predictions); thorough testing — correctness, integration, **load testing**; publish output formats/usage guidelines and train stakeholders.
- **Worked example (lesson 46):** sentiment API — endpoints receive text inputs, pass them through the trained model, return sentiment predictions; correctness/reliability/performance testing plus integration and load testing in the production environment.

## Section 7. Monitoring and maintenance (lessons 47–51)

- **Metrics & alerting (lesson 47):** choose business-anchored KPIs — accuracy/precision/recall/F1/AUC, RMSE, plus inference latency, throughput, error rates, prediction-distribution shape; set concrete targets ("99.5% or 99.9% accuracy based on business requirements"); **alerts when metrics deviate**; log every prediction, input and metadata; keep an audit trail of model versions.
- **Data-drift detection (lesson 48):** compare incoming vs. historical distributions — **statistical tests** (t-test, z-test, **KS test**, **KL divergence**), **visualizations** (histograms — shape/spread/peak shifts, density plots of the PDF, scatterplots of feature-pair correlation changes), **drift-detection models** (decision trees on incoming data; monitor rising error rates), **density-based methods** (DBSCAN/GMM clustering incoming vs. historical — different clusters = drift), **feature-drift monitoring** (mean, std, skewness, kurtosis per critical feature); then **set thresholds/tolerances from domain knowledge and trigger alerts** when exceeded.
- **Concept/model-drift detection (lesson 49):** track changes in performance metrics (accuracy, F1); classifier-based drift detection; monitor **residuals/prediction errors** on new data; **ensemble monitoring** — models with different algorithms/hyperparameters/features, measure agreement/consensus, discrepancies signal drift, reweight the ensemble; **adaptive monitoring strategies** that adjust thresholds and sensitivity to context; **feedback loops** — validate predictions (incl. human review), identify misclassifications, trigger corrective action or retraining.
- **Retraining & operations (lesson 50):** periodic evaluation pipeline against benchmarks using hold-out or **A/B testing**; incremental learning; **retraining intervals** driven by data-arrival rate and measured degradation; **version control for models, data and configurations** with rollback; model-management platforms for multiple models; interpret results, diagnose, and communicate findings/decisions.
- **Worked example (lesson 51): social-media monitoring model.** Per-sentiment accuracy/precision/recall/F1 thresholds; real-time prediction logging; KS tests + histogram/density-plot drift views; concept-drift detection; ensemble cross-checks; alerts on threshold breaches; human validation loop; adaptive thresholds/sensitivity; scheduled retraining triggered automatically on performance degradation; monitoring wired into the deployment pipeline; documented procedures, thresholds, actions and periodic performance/drift reports.

## Section 8. Collaboration (lessons 52–54)

- **People (lesson 52):** collaborate with **data scientists, software engineers, domain experts** and other stakeholders; ensure team diversity; regular meetings, stand-ups and workshops on progress; jointly gather/preprocess data with attention to quality, integrity and privacy; with domain experts, prioritize features and functional requirements.
- **Engineering collaboration (lesson 53):** shared repositories, track changes, code review; coding standards, documentation practices, best practices; **CI/CD pipelines** automating testing, building and deployment.
- **Worked example (lesson 54): predictive maintenance in a manufacturing facility** — cross-functional team tackled equipment downtime under constraints of data availability, prediction accuracy and integration with existing systems; prototype in a test environment; software engineers integrated the model into **SCADA and maintenance-management software**; post-deployment the trio keeps analyzing feedback and iterating the solution.

## Section 9. Research and innovation (lessons 55–57)

- **Method (lessons 55–56):** define research objectives/goals (tied to the AI system or problem) → comprehensive **literature review** of recent advancements → identify research areas (emerging technologies, novel algorithms, interdisciplinary approaches — or concrete company problems) → formulate **hypotheses/research questions** with expected benefits → design experiments (metrics, benchmark datasets) to validate → document findings (papers, technical reports, conference presentations) → share code/datasets via repositories and collaborative platforms → **collaborate with academics/industry** for diverse perspectives and replication → continuous learning via conferences, workshops and publications, refining methods from feedback.
- **Worked example (lesson 57): NLP text-classification research.** Reviewed traditional ML (SVM, Naive Bayes) vs. deep learning (CNN, RNN, Transformers) and current limits (scalability to large volumes, compute time/complexity); hypotheses: **attention mechanisms improve accuracy** by focusing on relevant text spans, and **model compression cuts compute without significant accuracy loss**; experiments measured accuracy/precision/recall/F1 on benchmark datasets (sentiment analysis, topic classification, document categorization); findings written into a paper submitted to conferences/journals, peer-reviewed, replicated with research groups, feeding back into continued learning.

## Section 10. Ethical considerations (lessons 58–60)

- **Foundations (lesson 58):** educate yourself and your team on implications; anchor to published codes — **ACM Code of Ethics, European Commission AI ethics guidelines**; bake ethics into the development process.
- **Bias:** run **bias audits**, use **fairness-aware ML**, and fairness metrics (**disparate impact, equalized odds, demographic parity**); mitigation across the pipeline — pre-processing (clean/rebalance data), in-processing (modify algorithms/architectures, **counterfactual fairness**, fairness constraints/penalties on discriminatory behavior), **post-processing** (adjust predictions).
- **Explainability, privacy, equity (lesson 58):** keep systems explainable/interpretable so decisions can be understood and bias detected; privacy techniques — **data anonymization, encryption, differential privacy**; prioritize fairness, equity and inclusivity; conduct **risk assessments** for unintended consequences with mitigation strategies; monitor ethical performance post-deployment; solicit stakeholder feedback; establish **ethics committees / advisory boards** with accountability and oversight.
- **Ethics by design (lesson 59):** two methodologies — the **Ethical Design Canvas** and **Value Sensitive Design (VSD)** (human-centered; identify implicated values — privacy, autonomy, fairness, dignity); then privacy-preserving techniques, bias audits and fairness-aware ML; governance structures (ethics committees, review boards, defined roles); **user empowerment** — informed consent for collection/processing/algorithmic decisions, access to review and correct their data and decisions; continuous post-deployment evaluation of fairness/transparency/accountability and iteration on evolving standards.
- **Worked example (lesson 60): an AI recruitment platform.** Assessed algorithmic-decision impact on **marginalized groups**; applied fairness-aware ML to reduce bias in screening/decisions; explainability so stakeholders understood the decisions; regular fairness audits; strong applicant-data privacy with **consent before collection**; continuous evaluation for fairness/transparency/effectiveness with feedback from applicants, hiring managers and diversity experts; an **ethics committee + advisory board** reviewing algorithm design, data usage and policy → harms mitigated, fairness promoted, stakeholder trust built.

## Section 11. Roadmap and summary (lessons 61–62)

- **Roadmap (lesson 61):** master **supervised, unsupervised and reinforcement learning** plus neural networks (CNN, RNN, transformers); keep up with research through papers, conferences, online courses; build **data-handling** skill (feature engineering, data manipulation, dimensionality reduction — much of it covered earlier in this course); learn to build and optimize algorithms — optimization techniques, parallel computing, distributed systems for scale; improve **collaboration and communication**, especially explaining technical concepts to non-technical stakeholders; follow tools/technologies continuously; prioritize **ethical and responsible AI** — fairness, transparency, accountability, privacy — and advocate for responsible practices.
